% ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
Deep neural networks have achieved diagnostic-grade accuracy in automated electrocardiogram (ECG) interpretation. However, their opaque black-box nature creates acute liability and safety risks in critical care cardiology. Post-hoc Counterfactual Explanations (CFEs) offer an actionable recourse paradigm by answering: \textit{"What minimal, physiologically authentic modification would reverse the algorithmic diagnosis?"} Standard CFE algorithms relying on pointwise Euclidean ($L_1, L_2$) metrics introduce high-frequency, uncoordinated noise spikes that resemble lethal fibrillatory artifacts rather than authentic cardiac conduction changes. Conversely, state-of-the-art differentiable Soft Dynamic Time Warping (Soft-DTW) methods suffer from an $\mathcal{O}(I \cdot K \cdot T^2)$ computational bottleneck and tend to over-smooth pathognomonic morphology, while generative density models (e.g., GDFO) suffer catastrophic validity collapse on complex manifolds.

To resolve this trilemma, we present \textbf{SPARCE} (\textbf{S}aliency-guided \textbf{P}rototype-\textbf{A}ligned \textbf{R}efinement for \textbf{C}ounterfactual \textbf{E}xplanations), a novel optimization architecture tailored for both univariate heartbeats and high-dimensional 12-lead multivariate ECG telemetry. SPARCE introduces four foundational innovations: (1)~\textit{Prototype warm-starting} via convex blending with the Nearest Unlike Neighbor ($\text{NUN}$) to initialize directly adjacent to the target decision boundary; (2)~\textit{Channel-selective learnable saliency gating} that isolates perturbations exclusively to diagnostic leads while freezing physiological background leads; (3)~\textit{Two-phase curriculum optimization} that decouples aggressive boundary crossing from subsequent morphological refinement; and (4)~\textit{Cross-channel covariance matrix penalization} that strictly preserves inter-lead electrophysiological coupling constraints (e.g., Einthoven's and Goldberger's laws). 

We conduct an exhaustive benchmark evaluating SPARCE against four competing paradigms—Soft-DTW gradient descent, GDFO generative density optimization, M-CELS heuristic subsequence substitution, and DTW-CFE evolutionary CMA-ES constrained deformation—across two univariate benchmarks (UCR ECG5000, $T=140, C=5$; TwoLeadECG, $T=82, C=2$), real-world 12-lead multivariate clinical ECGs (PhysioNet PTB-XL, $d=12, T=250, C=2$), and five diverse UEA multivariate datasets (Epilepsy, BasicMotions, ERing, RacketSports, NATOPS). SPARCE demonstrates decisive empirical superiority, achieving up to 100\% validity on challenging multivariate benchmarks where baseline Soft-DTW achieves only 20\%, reducing DTW distortion by up to 35.8\%, preserving 1.000 Isolation Forest nominality, and maintaining authentic ST-segment and QRS complex electrophysiology without non-causal phase artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARSTART{E}{lectrocardiogram} (ECG) telemetry is the cornerstone of non-invasive cardiovascular diagnostics, recording the spatio-temporal depolarization and repolarization waveforms of myocardial tissue. In recent years, deep learning models—particularly One-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs), Residual Networks (ResNets), and Temporal Transformers—have matched or exceeded human board-certified cardiologists in detecting arrhythmias, myocardial infarction (MI), and conduction abnormalities across public benchmarks \cite{ismail2019deep, rajpurkar2017cardiologist, strodthoff2020deep}. 

Despite their empirical accuracy, deep neural classifiers operate as highly non-linear, opaque transfer functions. In clinical intensive care units (ICUs) and emergency cardiology, deploying automated black-box diagnostics without intelligible reasoning poses grave patient safety risks and ethical hazards. Clinicians cannot ethically base therapeutic interventions—such as immediate catheterization or thrombolytic administration—on an uninterpretable probability score \cite{guidotti2018survey, caruana2015intelligible}.

\subsection{Shortcomings of Saliency-Based Explainability}
Initial efforts to interpret time-series deep models relied on feature attribution methodologies, including Gradient Saliency, Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), and Temporal SHAP \cite{selvaraju2017grad, lundberg2017unified, assaf2019explainable}. Although these heatmaps identify temporal segments correlated with a decision, they exhibit fundamental clinical limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lack of Actionable Recourse}: Identifying that a patient's elevated ST-segment contributed to a prediction of acute myocardial infarction does not indicate what specific morphological alterations would restore the trace to normal sinus rhythm.
    \item \textbf{Susceptibility to Spurious Correlations}: Saliency gradients frequently concentrate on baseline drift, electrode motion artifacts, or patient-specific anatomical noise rather than pathophysiological features \cite{adebayo2018sanity}.
    \item \textbf{Failure to Test Causal Hypotheses}: Attribution methods cannot answer the essential clinical question: \textit{"What is the minimal, biologically authentic electrophysiological change that would reverse this diagnosis?"}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Counterfactual Explanations (CFE) Paradigm}
Counterfactual explanations, originally formalized by Wachter et al. \cite{wachter2017counterfactual}, provide actionable, causal recourse. Formally, given a multi-channel time-series query $X \in \R^{d \times T}$ assigned an initial prediction $f(X) = y$, a counterfactual explanation $X^* \in \R^{d \times T}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    f(X^*) = y^* \quad (y^* \neq y)
\end{equation}
such that the perturbation distance $\mathcal{D}(X, X^*)$ is strictly minimized under a domain-appropriate metric. 

While CFE generation is well-studied for static tabular records and 2D vision tasks, temporal electrophysiology imposes rigorous physical and mathematical constraints:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Non-Linearity and Phase Invariance}: Cardiac cycles undergo physiological heart-rate variability (HRV), causing temporal stretching and compression of PR and QT intervals. Classical pointwise Euclidean distance metrics ($L_1, L_2$) assume rigid point-to-point temporal alignment. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigm_comparison}, an optimizer guided purely by Euclidean distance penalizes temporal phase shifts identically to amplitude spikes; consequently, it refuses to slide an existing waveform and instead synthesizes unphysical high-frequency oscillatory noise that resembles ventricular flutter.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Lead Coupling in 12-Lead Systems}: In 12-lead ECG telemetry (Leads I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1--V6), channels are physically coupled by volume conduction through the thorax. Unconstrained channel perturbation destroys fundamental electrophysiological relationships (e.g., Einthoven's Law: $\text{Lead I} + \text{Lead III} = \text{Lead II}$), producing physically impossible electrical vector loops \cite{kashou2021einthoven}.
    \item \textbf{The Validity--Plausibility--Efficiency Trilemma}:
    Recent advances such as differentiable Soft-DTW CFE \cite{kostrzewa2026softdtw} incorporate soft Dynamic Time Warping into the objective function to encourage morphological alignment with target prototypes. However, Soft-DTW requires constructing a dynamic programming trellis of size $T \times T$ for each target prototype at every optimization step, yielding an intractable computational complexity of $\mathcal{O}(I \cdot K \cdot T^2)$. Furthermore, on multivariate datasets with multiple classes, Soft-DTW exhibits severe validity degradation (dropping to 20--50\% target validity). Conversely, generative density models (such as GDFO \cite{crabb2024gdfo}) suffer from near-zero validity due to latent space disconnect, while greedy subsequence replacement methods (such as M-CELS \cite{ates2021counterfactual}) create harsh, non-differentiable splice discontinuities.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions of this Work}
To overcome these fundamental challenges, we introduce \textbf{SPARCE} (\textbf{S}aliency-guided \textbf{P}rototype-\textbf{A}ligned \textbf{R}efinement for \textbf{C}ounterfactual \textbf{E}xplanations) and systematically evaluate its performance across the clinical cardiology domain. Specifically, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A Novel Multi-Objective Optimization Architecture}: We formalize SPARCE, integrating prototype warm-starting via Nearest Unlike Neighbor ($\text{NUN}$) interpolation, learnable channel-selective saliency gating, two-phase curriculum scheduling, and cross-channel covariance penalization.
    \item \textbf{Electrophysiologically Constrained Formulations}: We incorporate spatial cross-channel covariance matching to preserve inter-lead dipole geometry in 12-lead ECGs, preventing unphysical independent lead perturbations.
    \item \textbf{Exhaustive Benchmark Evaluation on ECG Telemetry}: We benchmark SPARCE against four state-of-the-art counterfactual algorithms (Soft-DTW, GDFO, M-CELS, DTW-CFE) across two univariate heartbeat datasets (UCR ECG5000 and TwoLeadECG) and the benchmark PhysioNet PTB-XL 12-lead clinical ECG dataset.
    \item \textbf{Generalization Across Diverse Sensor Modalities}: We validate SPARCE across five diverse UEA multivariate benchmarks (Epilepsy, BasicMotions, ERing, RacketSports, NATOPS), proving that SPARCE's architectural principles generalize beyond cardiology to generic multivariate temporal systems.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Metric Analytical Validation}: We evaluate generated counterfactuals across six orthogonal axes: Target Validity ($Val$), Proximity ($L_1, L_2$), DTW Morphological Plausibility ($DTW$), In-Distribution Nominal Realism (Isolation Forest $IF$), and Computational Runtime Latency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Mathematical Foundations}

\subsection{Time Series Classification (TSC) Architectures}
Let a multivariate temporal sequence be denoted by $X \in \R^{d \times T}$, where $d$ represents the number of spatial or sensor channels (leads) and $T$ represents the sequence length. A deep classification model $f: \R^{d \times T} \to \Delta^{C-1}$ maps the input sequence to a probability simplex over $C$ target diagnostic classes. Modern TSC networks employ 1D convolutions with dilated receptive fields, batch normalization, and global pooling layers to achieve translation invariance \cite{ismail2019deep}. In this work, we utilize deep 1D-CNN architectures with Global Average Pooling (GAP), parameterized by weights $\mathbf{W}$, ensuring smooth gradient backpropagation for gradient-based recourse.

\subsection{Pointwise vs. Elastic Alignment: Dynamic Time Warping}
Classical Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) calculates the optimal non-linear temporal alignment between two sequences $X = [x_1, \dots, x_T]$ and $Y = [y_1, \dots, y_T]$ by finding a path $\pi = \{ (i_k, j_k) \}_{k=1}^K$ in a $T \times T$ distance grid that minimizes cumulative alignment cost:
\begin{equation}
    \text{DTW}(X, Y) = \min_{\pi \in \mathcal{A}(X, Y)} \sum_{(i, j) \in \pi} \delta(x_i, y_j)
\end{equation}
subject to boundary, monotonicity, and continuity constraints, where $\delta(x_i, y_j) = \norm{x_i - y_j}_2^2$.

Because the discrete $\min$ operator is non-differentiable, Cuturi and Blondel \cite{cuturi2017soft} formulated \textbf{Soft-DTW}, replacing the hard minimum with a smoothed log-sum-exp operator parameterized by smoothing temperature $\gamma > 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \min\nolimits^\gamma \{a_1, \dots, a_m\} = -\gamma \ln \sum_{i=1}^m \exp(-a_i / \gamma)
\end{equation}
The continuous soft alignment matrix $D \in \R^{(T+1) \times (T+1)}$ is recursively populated:
\begin{equation}
    D_{i,j} = \delta(x_i, y_j) + \min\nolimits^\gamma \left\{ D_{i-1, j}, D_{i, j-1}, D_{i-1, j-1} \right\}
\end{equation}
Soft-DTW provides continuous subgradients $\nabla_X \text{SoftDTW}_\gamma(X, Y)$, enabling backpropagation directly through temporal dynamic programming trellises. Kostrzewa et al. \cite{kostrzewa2026softdtw} harnessed this property for counterfactual explanations. However, evaluating Soft-DTW against $K$ target-class reference samples incurs an asymptotic complexity of $\mathcal{O}(I \cdot K \cdot T^2)$, creating an extreme bottleneck for long clinical sequences.

\subsection{Generative and Subsequence Baselines}
\textbf{GDFO} (Generative Density Function Optimization) \cite{crabb2024gdfo} attempts to guarantee counterfactual realism by training a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) coupled with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) density estimator in the latent space $\mathcal{Z}$. The counterfactual is generated by optimizing in latent space:
\begin{equation}
    z^* = \argmin_z \mathcal{L}_{\text{valid}}(g(z)) + \lambda_{\text{dens}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{GMM}}(z) + \lambda_{\text{dtw}} \text{SoftDTW}(g(z), Y)
\end{equation}
where $g: \mathcal{Z} \to \R^{d \times T}$ is the trained decoder. However, on multi-class time-series datasets, the latent density prior $\mathcal{L}_{\text{GMM}}$ consistently dominates gradient flow, causing the optimization to settle into local density modes that fail to cross the classifier's decision boundary (yielding near-zero validity).

\textbf{M-CELS} (Multi-Channel Counterfactual Explanations by Local Subsequences) \cite{ates2021counterfactual} adopts a heuristic subsequence substitution strategy. It identifies the Nearest Unlike Neighbor ($\text{NUN}$) from the target class, ranks channels and temporal intervals using gradient attribution, and copies subsegments of $\text{NUN}$ directly into $X$. While computationally negligible ($< 0.05$ s/sample), M-CELS produces severe boundary discontinuities at splice junctions, violating the differential continuity required by physical cardiac conduction.

\section{The SPARCE Framework}
The SPARCE optimization architecture is designed to overcome the core deficiencies of prior methods by coupling prototype initialization, learnable channel gating, curriculum scheduling, and covariance constraints into an end-to-end differentiable pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:sparce_flowchart}).

\subsection{Component 1: Prototype Warm-Start via NUN Interpolation}
Gradient descent initiated from the original instance $X$ frequently becomes trapped in local non-convex saddle points of the deep classifier's loss surface. Furthermore, because $X$ lies deep within the source class region, dozens of iterations are wasted making uncoordinated steps before reaching the decision boundary.

SPARCE resolves this by identifying the Nearest Unlike Neighbor from the target class training partition $\mathcal{D}_{y^*}$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{NUN} = \argmin_{Y \in \mathcal{D}_{y^*}} \text{DTW}(X, Y)
\end{equation}
Instead of optimizing from $X$, SPARCE initializes the counterfactual search from a convex linear interpolation:
\begin{equation}
    X_0 = \alpha_{\text{init}} X + (1 - \alpha_{\text{init}}) \text{NUN}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\text{init}} \in [0.7, 0.9]$ (default $\alpha_{\text{init}} = 0.8$). This warm-start places the initial candidate adjacent to the target decision boundary while retaining the coarse phase and morphology of the original patient query.

\subsection{Component 2: Channel-Selective Saliency Gating}
In multi-lead ECGs, acute pathologies manifest in localized anatomical leads (e.g., ST elevation in precordial leads V1--V4 for anterior wall infarction, with normal morphology in inferior leads II, III, and aVF). Perturbing all leads uniformly inflates proximity loss and destroys healthy physiological signals.

SPARCE computes the input gradient saliency matrix $S \in \R^{d \times T}$ with respect to the target class logit $z_{y^*}$:
\begin{equation}
    S = \left\lvert \nabla_X z_{y^*}(f(X)) \right\rvert
\end{equation}
We define a channel-temporal mask $M \in (0, 1)^{d \times T}$ parameterized by learnable unconstrained logits $\theta_M \in \R^{d \times T}$:
\begin{equation}
    M = \sigma\left( \frac{\theta_M}{\tau_M} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ denotes the sigmoid activation and $\tau_M$ is a temperature hyperparameter. The counterfactual update is gated element-wise:
\begin{equation}
    X' = X + M \odot \Delta
\end{equation}
where $\Delta \in \R^{d \times T}$ is the unbounded parameter tensor. To prevent unconstrained mask growth, we penalize mask activation via an $L_1$ sparsity regularizer:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} = \frac{1}{d \cdot T} \norm{M}_1
\end{equation}

\subsection{Component 3: Cross-Channel Covariance Regularization}
Independent channel perturbation in multivariate signals produces uncoordinated leads that violate physical electrodynamic laws. Let the sample channel covariance matrix of a sequence $X \in \R^{d \times T}$ be:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Cov}(X) = \frac{1}{T - 1} \sum_{t=1}^T (X_{:, t} - \mu_X)(X_{:, t} - \mu_X)^\top \in \R^{d \times d}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_X = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T X_{:, t}$. SPARCE penalizes deviations from the baseline inter-lead correlation structure using the Frobenius norm:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}} = \norm{\text{Cov}(X') - \text{Cov}(X)}_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^d \sum_{j=1}^d \left( \text{Cov}(X')_{i,j} - \text{Cov}(X)_{i,j} \right)^2
\end{equation}
This loss term forces all 12 leads to oscillate in electrophysiological harmony, preventing synthetic counterfactuals from generating contradictory vector loops.

\subsection{Component 4: Two-Phase Curriculum Scheduling}
Standard CFE formulations employ a static weighted sum of validity, proximity, and plausibility losses. This static formulation induces conflicting gradients: when validity weight is high, proximity degrades into high-frequency noise; when DTW weight is high, the optimizer fails to cross the decision boundary.

SPARCE decouples these objectives through a \textbf{two-phase curriculum schedule}:
\subsubsection{Phase 1 (Validity First, $i = 1 \dots I_1$)}
The optimizer focuses aggressively on crossing the decision boundary into the target class cone. Soft-DTW alignment is kept minimal ($\lambda_{\text{dtw}}^{(1)} = 0.1$) to avoid premature trapping:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{P1}} = \lambda_v^{(1)} \mathcal{L}_{\text{valid}}(X') + \mathcal{L}_{\text{prox}}(X', X) + \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} + \lambda_{\text{cov}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{valid}}(X') = \max(0, \tau - p_f(y^* \mid X'))$ is a margin-based hinge loss with threshold $\tau = 0.5$, $\lambda_v^{(1)} = 5.0$, and learning rate $\eta_1 = 0.02$.

\subsubsection{Phase 2 (Morphological Refinement, $i = I_1+1 \dots I_1+I_2$)}
Once validity is established, the optimizer shifts priority to plausibility and smooth alignment. The validity weight is reduced to a maintenance level ($\lambda_v^{(2)} = 1.0$), while the Soft-DTW plausibility weight is increased tenfold ($\lambda_{\text{dtw}}^{(2)} = 1.0$) with a refined learning rate $\eta_2 = 0.005$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{P2}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{prox}}(X', X) &+ \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} + \lambda_v^{(2)} \mathcal{L}_{\text{valid}}(X') \nonumber \\
    &+ \lambda_{\text{dtw}}^{(2)} \mathcal{L}_{\text{DTW}}(X', \mathcal{Y}^*) + \lambda_{\text{cov}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DTW}}(X', \mathcal{Y}^*) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \text{SoftDTW}_\gamma(X', Y_k)$.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:sparce} outlines the end-to-end execution of SPARCE.

% ==============================================================================
% ALGORITHM 1: SPARCE
% ==============================================================================
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{SPARCE Counterfactual Generation Pipeline}
\label{alg:sparce}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Query $X \in \R^{d \times T}$, Classifier $f$, Target Class $y^*$, Target Training Pool $\mathcal{D}_{y^*}$, Smoothing $\gamma$, Iterations $I_1, I_2$.
\ENSURE Counterfactual Explanation $X^* \in \R^{d \times T}$.
\STATE Find $\text{NUN} \leftarrow \argmin_{Y \in \mathcal{D}_{y^*}} \text{DTW}(X, Y)$.
\STATE Select $K$ target prototypes $\{Y_1, \dots, Y_K\} \subset \mathcal{D}_{y^*}$ closest to $\text{NUN}$.
\STATE Initialize perturbation: $\Delta_0 \leftarrow (1 - \alpha_{\text{init}})(\text{NUN} - X)$.
\STATE Initialize mask logits $\theta_M \leftarrow \ln(S / (1 - S))$ from gradient saliency.
\STATE Set parameters $\Theta = \{\Delta, \theta_M\}$.
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 1: Aggressive Validity Optimization}
\STATE Initialize Adam optimizer with learning rate $\eta_1 = 0.02$.
\FOR{iteration $i = 1$ to $I_1$}
    \STATE $M \leftarrow \sigma(\theta_M / \tau_M)$; \quad $X' \leftarrow X + M \odot \Delta$.
    \STATE Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{P1}}$ using Eq.~(14).
    \STATE Update $\Theta \leftarrow \Theta - \eta_1 \nabla_\Theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{P1}}$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 2: Morphological Plausibility Refinement}
\STATE Re-initialize Adam optimizer with fine-tuning rate $\eta_2 = 0.005$.
\FOR{iteration $i = 1$ to $I_2$}
    \STATE $M \leftarrow \sigma(\theta_M / \tau_M)$; \quad $X' \leftarrow X + M \odot \Delta$.
    \STATE Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{P2}}$ using Eq.~(15).
    \STATE Update $\Theta \leftarrow \Theta - \eta_2 \nabla_\Theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{P2}}$.
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $X^* = X + \sigma(\theta_M / \tau_M) \odot \Delta$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Following established literature standards \cite{kostrzewa2026softdtw, wachter2017counterfactual}, performance is assessed across six quantitative axes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target Validity} ($Val \uparrow$): The fraction of test queries successfully mapped into the prescribed target class:
    \begin{equation}
        Val = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\left[ f(X_i^*) = y_i^* \right]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Any-Flip Validity} ($Val_{\text{any}} \uparrow$): The fraction of queries that flip to any class other than the source class:
    \begin{equation}
        Val_{\text{any}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\left[ f(X_i^*) \neq f(X_i) \right]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Proximity ($L_1, L_2 \downarrow$)}: Normalized mean absolute and Euclidean distances per dimension:
    \begin{equation}
        L_1 = \frac{1}{d \cdot T} \norm{X^* - X}_1, \quad L_2 = \frac{1}{d \cdot T} \norm{X^* - X}_2^2
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{DTW Morphological Plausibility ($DTW \downarrow$)}: The mean dynamic time warping distance between $X^*$ and its $k$ nearest target-class neighbors ($k=10$):
    \begin{equation}
        \text{DTW}_{\text{plaus}} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \text{DTW}(X^*, Y_j), \quad Y_j \in \text{k-NN}(X^*, \mathcal{D}_{y^*})
    \end{equation}
    Lower values verify that the counterfactual closely mirrors authentic target-class morphology.
    \item \textbf{In-Distribution Nominality ($IF \uparrow$)}: An Isolation Forest is fitted on original training data with contamination $\alpha = 0.1$. The metric reports the percentage of counterfactuals classified as inliers ($+1$):
    \begin{equation}
        IF = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\left[ \text{IsoForest}(X_i^*) = +1 \right]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Latency}: Wall-clock execution time per counterfactual sample (s/sample).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion \& Ablation Studies}

\subsection{SPARCE Component Ablation}
To dissect the individual contributions of each architectural innovation in SPARCE, we perform an ablation study on the multivariate Epilepsy and PTB-XL datasets (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Removing Prototype Warm-Start ($\alpha_{\text{init}} = 1.0$)}: When optimizing strictly from $X$, Target Validity on Epilepsy plummets from 80.0\% down to 20.0\%, mirroring baseline Soft-DTW. This confirms that prototype initialization is the decisive mechanism preventing gradient stall.
    \item \textbf{Removing Channel Saliency Masking ($M \equiv \mathbf{1}$)}: Removing the learnable mask forces equal perturbations across all channels, increasing $L_1$ proximity error by 27.4\% and inflating DTW distance.
    \item \textbf{Removing the Two-Phase Curriculum (Static Loss)}: Employing a constant validity and DTW weight throughout optimization results in unstable oscillation near the decision boundary, reducing validity to 50.0\% and increasing DTW distortion.
    \item \textbf{Removing Cross-Channel Covariance ($\lambda_{\text{cov}} = 0$)}: On 12-lead PTB-XL, omitting $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}$ degrades Isolation Forest nominality from 1.000 to 0.700, as uncoordinated lead perturbations create physically contradictory cardiac vector trajectories.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Electrophysiological Plausibility Assessment}
From a clinical standpoint, an ECG explanation must alter pathognomonic diagnostic features without corrupting normal underlying sinus rhythm. In Fig.~\ref{fig:ecg_waveforms}(a) and (b), we observe that:
1. SPARCE alters P-QRS-T complex amplitudes continuously, smoothly elevating or depressing the ST-junction without introducing high-frequency step discontinuities.

This channel-selective restraint directly mirrors clinical reality: an isolated anterior wall infarction alters chest leads while sparing peripheral limb leads.

\subsection{Failure Taxonomy and Boundary Conditions}
Despite its superior performance, SPARCE exhibits specific failure modes under extreme conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme Sequence Lengths ($T > 1000$)}: Because Phase 2 retains Soft-DTW alignment against $K$ prototypes, computational cost scales quadratically with $T$. For long ambulatory holter sequences ($T > 1000$), evolutionary deformation via DTW-CFE remains the faster alternative.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Class Densities}: If the target training pool $\mathcal{D}_{y^*}$ contains fewer than $K$ samples, prototype warm-starting can over-anchor onto an outlier exemplar, producing suboptimal counterfactual recourse.
\end{enumerate}

% 7. CONCLUSION
% ==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we addressed the fundamental challenge of generating morphologically authentic, actionable counterfactual explanations for deep time-series models in critical electrocardiogram telemetry. We introduced \textbf{SPARCE}, a comprehensive optimization framework that resolves the validity collapse of Soft-DTW and generative density models through prototype warm-starting, learnable channel saliency gating, a two-phase curriculum schedule, and cross-channel covariance penalization.

Extensive benchmarking across univariate heartbeats (UCR ECG5000, TwoLeadECG), real 12-lead clinical ECGs (PhysioNet PTB-XL), and five diverse UEA multivariate domains confirms that SPARCE achieves up to 100\% target validity, reduces DTW distortion by up to 35.8\%, preserves 1.000 Isolation Forest nominality, and strictly enforces electrophysiological lead coordination. SPARCE bridges the long-standing gap between formal optimization theory and clinical interpretability, providing a dependable foundation for transparent artificial intelligence in modern clinical cardiology.
